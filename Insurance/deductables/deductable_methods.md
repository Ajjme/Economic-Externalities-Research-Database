The Carrier's Mandate (The Percentage Trap)
For wind, hail, or named storms, insurance companies often take the choice out of your hands. In areas prone to severe weather, insurers mandate a percentage-based deductible rather than a flat dollar amount. This percentage usually ranges from 1% to 5% of your home's total insured dwelling value (Coverage A), not the cost of the damage.  

Because of this, two next-door neighbors in the exact same zip code can have vastly different out-of-pocket costs for the same hailstorm:
Neighbor A has a home insured for $300,000 with a mandatory 2% wind/hail deductible. Their out-of-pocket cost before insurance pays a dime is $6,000.
Neighbor B has a larger home insured for $500,000 with the same 2% mandate. Their out-of-pocket cost is $10,000.
Because the actual dollar value of the deductible is tied directly to individual home replacement costs rather than a flat regional rate, data aggregators cannot publish a meaningful "average zip code deductible."
How Deductibles and Premiums Interact
Think of premiums and deductibles as a financial see-saw. The premium is your guaranteed cost (what you pay to keep the lights on), while the deductible is your speculative cost (what you pay only if disaster strikes).

The "Overall Cost" Math
To find the true financial impact, you have to look at your risk over a timeline (e.g., 5 to 10 years).
If raising your wind/hail deductible saves you $400 a year in premiums, but increases your out-of-pocket claim cost by $4,000, you have to go 10 years without a single hail claim to break even. If your region gets hit by severe convective storms every 3 to 4 years, paying the higher premium for a lower deductible often ends up costing less in the long run.
The Wind, Hail, and Roof Nuance
When looking at multi-hazard policies, you need to watch out for Split Deductibles. A standard modern policy rarely has just one deductible. It is typically broken down into two distinct categories on your declarations page:  
Bluefield Realty Group
All Other Perils (AOP): Applies to fire, burst pipes, or vandalism. This is usually a predictable, flat dollar amount (e.g., $1,000).  
Insureon
Wind/Hail or Named Storm: Supersedes the AOP deductible the moment a storm damages your roof or siding. This is where the percentage-based calculation kicks in.
The Roof Depreciation Variable
Even if you navigate the deductible premium trade-off perfectly, the way your policy treats your roof can completely alter your actual costs. You must check whether your roof is covered under Replacement Cost Value (RCV) or Actual Cash Value (ACV).
If a storm hits a 15-year-old roof under an ACV policy, the insurer will depreciate the payout based on the roof's age before subtracting your percentage deductible. This double-hit is why many homeowners find themselves facing massive out-of-pocket gaps when trying to fund a replacement after a storm.
